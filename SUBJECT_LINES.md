# Qualtrics Subject Line Analysis & Templates
### What gets cold emails opened — from the 382 source emails + Lavender, Gong, 30MPC, and Outreach data

> Companion to `WINNING_TEMPLATES.md`. A great email never gets read if the subject line dies in the
> inbox. This file analyzes the subject lines that worked, layers in the external research the user
> asked for, and gives **plug-and-play subject-line templates + real examples for each framework.**

## Part 1 — What the source doc shows

43 of the 382 winners carried a logged subject line. The pattern is remarkably consistent and — it
turns out — already aligned with what the big external studies recommend:

| Metric (source doc) | Value | External best-practice target |
|---|---|---|
| **Median length** | **4 words / 27 characters** | Lavender: 4–7 words / 36–50 chars · Gong/30MPC: 1–4 words |
| 1–3 words | 49% of subjects | "shorter = higher open" (Gong) |
| All-lowercase style | 16% (and rising in recent ones) | Lavender + Gong: lowercase wins |
| Ends in "?" | 5% | Lavender: questions hurt cold opens |
| Contains "Qualtrics" | 44% | Fine when **warm**; avoid when **cold** (see Part 3) |
| Uses a connector (`<>`, `|`, `/`) | 23% | "internal camouflage" — looks like an internal thread |

**Top real subject lines from the data**, already matching best practice:

`ux & design feedback loop` · `Omnichannel Intelligence` · `new AI features <> Qualtrics` ·
`Shari Llona conversations <> Qualtrics` · `New to Pittsburgh!`

## Part 2 — What the external research says (Lavender · Gong · 30MPC · Outreach)

**The strong consensus across all four sources:**

1. **Short wins.** Gong/30MPC (85M+ emails): **1–4 words**, shorter = higher open. Lavender (500M+
   emails): the response-rate sweet spot is **36–50 characters (~4–7 words)**. Outreach: 1–8 words.
2. **Lowercase wins.** All four favor all-lowercase (proper nouns excepted) — it reads like a note
   from a colleague, not a campaign.
3. **"Internal camouflage" is the single biggest idea (Gong/30MPC).** Mirror how the buyer's own
   coworkers email them: short noun phrases like *"trial delays," "hiring ops," "2024 forecasts."*
   The subject should look like it came from inside the building.
4. **Don't sell or buzzword in the subject (Gong).** Pitching reduces reply rates by **up to 57%**.
   Avoid marketing language — and Gong specifically says **don't say "AI"** or use buzzwords in the
   subject line.
5. **Personalize.** Outreach/Lavender: personalized subjects lift opens **22–50%** (deep
   personalization 40%+). Reference *their* company, role, or a recent event — not your product.
6. **Subject line + first line of the body must work as a pair (30MPC).** The subject sets up the
   opener; don't waste the subject repeating the opener.

**Two real tensions in the data — resolved by COLD vs. WARM:**

| Lever | "Use it" camp | "Avoid it" camp | How to resolve |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Numbers** | Outreach: numbers +37% opens | Lavender: numbers −46% opens in **cold** | Skip numbers in cold prospecting; fine in warm/nurture where a metric is the hook |
| **Questions** | Outreach: questions ~+10% opens; "Connect?" hit 71% | Lavender: questions −56% opens in **cold** | Avoid "?" in cold (F3/F4-cold); OK in **warm** (F1, F2, referral) where a relationship already exists |

> **Benchmark (Lavender):** good cold open rate = **25–40%**, excellent = **40%+**, below 15% means
> the subject line or deliverability is broken.

## Part 3 — The 6 golden rules (synthesized)

1. **≤ 4 words for cold, ≤ 6 for warm.** If it wraps on mobile, it's too long.
2. **all lowercase** (except the prospect's name, company, or a proper noun).
3. **No selling, no "AI," no buzzwords, no exclamation hype.** Pitching in the subject tanks replies.
4. **Cold = internal camouflage (no "Qualtrics").** Warm = you *may* use "Qualtrics / [Company]"
   because the relationship makes it look like a real thread.
5. **One specific, true noun phrase** — their initiative, their metric, a referrer, a trigger.
6. **No "?" or numbers in cold.** Save questions for warm/referral threads.

## Part 4 — Subject-line templates + examples by framework

Each framework gets: the **strategy**, **plug-and-play templates**, **real examples from the source
doc**, and **A/B partners** to test against each other.

---

### Framework 1 — "You Already Use Us" (Warm Expansion)
**Strategy:** the relationship is the hook. Because they're a customer, naming "Qualtrics / [Company]"
looks like a legitimate internal thread, not a cold pitch. Connectors (`<>`, `|`, `+`) reinforce that.

**Templates**
```
[Company] <> Qualtrics
[Company] + Qualtrics partnership
your [Company] license
[team] @ [Company] <> Qualtrics
quick one on [Company]'s setup
```
**Real examples from the source doc**
> `Welcome to Shamrock / Qualtrics partnership` · `Qualtrics | ex insights` · `Consolidation` ·
> `new AI features <> Qualtrics` · `Qualtrics Current Partnership`

**A/B partners:** `[Company] <> Qualtrics` (relationship) vs. `quick one on [Company]'s setup`
(curiosity). *Note: `Consolidation` is the strongest performer here — a single internal-sounding noun.*

---

### Framework 2 — "Reconnect / New POC" (Re-engagement)
**Strategy:** a prior relationship earns a warm, low-key subject — and is the one place a **question**
is allowed (you've met, so it reads as a genuine follow-up, not bait).

**Templates**
```
reconnecting
picking back up — [Company]
[Company] <> Qualtrics
circling back on [project]
did you get my voicemail?     ← question OK here (warm)
new POC for [Company]
```
**Real examples from the source doc**
> `Qualtrics reconnect` · `Reconnecting: Employee experience updates` · `Did you get my Voicemail?` ·
> `Future alignment with Visa`

**A/B partners:** `reconnecting` (plain) vs. `circling back on [project]` (specific recall). The
specific-recall version mirrors the body's "based on my notes…" and usually wins.

---

### Framework 3 — "Role + Peer Proof" (Cold Outreach)
**Strategy:** this is pure cold — apply the rules hardest. **No "Qualtrics," no pitch, 1–3 lowercase
words, internal camouflage.** Name *their* initiative or metric as if a coworker flagged it.

**Templates**
```
[their initiative]            → e.g. "member insights"
[their metric]                → e.g. "CAHPS scores"
[peer] + [their company]      → e.g. "Citi + your CX program"
quick question, [first name]
[domain] benchmarks
[team] priorities
```
**Real examples from the source doc**
> `member insights program` · `digital experience planning` · `marketing insights alignment` ·
> `ux & design feedback loop` · `Omnichannel Intelligence` · `cx platform comparison`

**A/B partners:** `member insights` (their initiative) vs. `Citi + your program` (peer proof). For
displacement plays, test the incumbent as the hook: `medallia vs. qualtrics` — short, neutral, opens.
*Reminder: do **not** put "AI" in a cold subject (Gong) — save it for the body.*

---

### Framework 4 — "Trigger Strike" (Intent / Signal)
**Strategy:** the trigger is the most personalized hook you'll ever have — use it literally. Reference
the demo, the visit, the article, or the new role. Specificity drives the open; keep selling out.

**Templates**
```
re: your [demo / visit]
following your [Becker's article / report]
congrats — [Company]
your [initiative] at [Company]
[topic] from your [podcast/article]
```
**Real examples from the source doc**
> `insurance experience alignment` · `Reconnecting: Employee experience updates` ·
> `Exciting news: Qualtrics/Hyundai partnership` · `Shari Llona conversations <> Qualtrics`

**A/B partners:** `re: your demo` (mirrors an internal reply) vs. `congrats — [Company]` (new-role
trigger). The `re:`-style subject leans into internal camouflage and tends to over-perform on opens —
just make sure the body honestly references the trigger.

---

### Framework 5 — "Borrowed Trust" (Referral / On-site)
**Strategy:** the **referrer's name** is the highest-trust word you can put in an inbox — lead with
it. For on-sites, the **city + timing** creates the open-worthy hook.

**Templates**
```
[Referrer] suggested I reach out
[Referrer] → [first name]
[Referrer]'s intro
in [City] [date]
[City] next week
coffee while we're in [City]?     ← question OK (warm-ish, low stakes)
```
**Real examples from the source doc**
> `New to Pittsburgh!` · `A quick intro to AJ + A Raleigh hello`

**A/B partners:** `[Referrer] suggested I reach out` (full context) vs. `[Referrer] → you` (ultra-short
internal style). When the referrer is senior/known, the short version wins — the name does all the work.

## Part 5 — Quick cheat sheet

| Framework | Default go-to subject | Cold/Warm | "?" allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 Expansion | `[Company] <> Qualtrics` | Warm | Yes |
| F2 Reconnect | `circling back on [project]` | Warm | Yes |
| F3 Cold | `[their initiative]` (e.g. `member insights`) | **Cold** | No |
| F4 Trigger | `re: your [trigger]` | Cold/Warm | Sparingly |
| F5 Borrowed Trust | `[Referrer] suggested I reach out` | Warm | Yes |

### Do / Don't
✅ all lowercase · ≤4 words cold · their initiative/metric/referrer · looks like an internal note ·
subject + first line as a pair
❌ "Qualtrics" in cold subjects · "AI" or buzzwords · selling/pitching · numbers in cold · "?" in cold ·
ALL CAPS or exclamation hype · generic "Touching base" / "Following up"

### Test like the pros
Pick **one variable** (length, personalization, or tone), send the two A/B partners listed under each
framework to a 20–30 prospect split, and keep the winner. Lavender benchmark to beat: **25–40% open**.
